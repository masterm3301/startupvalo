
Building an AI that mimics how VCs and Angel investors value startups is a great "scrapper" project—it's essentially a logic engine that weighs subjective "vibe" metrics against hard data.

In a CrewAI setup, you wouldn't just have one agent; you’d have a "Investment Committee" where each agent looks for different "red flags" and "green flags."

Here are 5 agent scenarios for a Startup Valuation Crew:

1. The "Skin in the Game" Auditor
Investors rarely value a startup just on the idea; they value the execution. This agent focuses on the Founding Team.

Role: Lead Talent Scout.

Goal: Research the founders' digital footprint (LinkedIn, GitHub, past exits).

Analysis: It looks for "Product-Market-Founder Fit." Did the founder build something similar before? Do they have technical "deep-work" proof?

Valuation Logic: Adds a "Multiplier" to the valuation if the founders have a history of shipping fast; applies a "Discount" for solo founders with no technical background.

2. The "TAM/SAM/SOM" Reality Checker
Most founders hallucinate their market size. This agent is the Skeptical Economist.

Role: Market Size Validator.

Goal: Scrape recent industry reports and competitor filings to find the actual addressable market.

Analysis: If the founder says "The market is $10B," this agent cross-references to see if that includes unrelated sectors.

Valuation Logic: Calculates a valuation based on a "Multiple" of the Realistic Obtainable Market (SOM) rather than the "Total" market.

3. The "Moat & defensibility" Strategist
This agent thinks like a competitor. It tries to figure out how easy it is to "kill" this startup.

Role: Competitive Intelligence Officer.

Goal: Identify direct and indirect competitors.

Analysis: Does the startup have a "Moat"? (e.g., Proprietary tech, network effects, or high switching costs).

Valuation Logic: It assigns a "Defensibility Score." High defensibility (like a unique patent or a "scrappy" distribution edge) significantly bumps the valuation.

4. The "Unit Economics" Stress-Tester
This agent is the CFO. It ignores the "vision" and looks only at the math of the business model.

Role: Financial Analyst.

Goal: Analyze the Burn Rate, Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), and Lifetime Value (LTV).

Analysis: If the startup is spending $10 to make $5, this agent flags it as a "Leaky Bucket."

Valuation Logic: Uses a Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) model or Comparable Company Analysis (CCA) to provide a valuation based on actual or projected revenue multiples.

5. The "Hype & Sentiment" Pulse
Valuations are often driven by FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out). This agent gauges the Market Temperature.

Role: Sentiment Analyst.

Goal: Scrape Twitter (X), TechCrunch, and Y-Combinator forums to see if the "Sector" (e.g., AI Agents, CleanTech) is currently "Hot."

Analysis: Is the sector currently receiving massive VC inflows? What are the current "pre-money" averages for this stage?

Valuation Logic: Applies a "Hype Premium." If everyone is investing in AI Agents right now, the valuation increases simply because the market is willing to pay more.

How they work together (The Crew Process)
Input: You give the Crew a Pitch Deck URL or a description.

Collaboration: The Market Checker finds the data → The Financial Analyst builds the model → The Founding Auditor adjusts for risk.

Final Task: A "Manager Agent" takes all 5 reports and outputs a Final Investment Memo with a suggested valuation range (e.g., "$2M - $2.5M Pre-money").

Would you want the final output to be a formal VC-style memo, or a "Scrappy" breakdown of why the idea might fail?
